## A1. Objectives of the Doctor of Engineering in Engineering

Penn State, long respected and consistently recognized for having one of the top engineering programs in the country, is proud to offer a high-quality online Doctor of Engineering in Engineering (D.Eng.) program through the Penn State World Campus. Taught by respected Penn State faculty with strong academic credentials and real-world experience, the online D.Eng. program can position you for advancement to the highest-level leadership positions in research and engineering in both the private and public sectors. As a D.Eng. student, you can learn to:

- research and develop new products or processes that can benefit industrial, governmental, or military entities;
- analyze and synthesize critical information within your discipline, and, where appropriate, across multiple disciplines;
- communicate the major issues of your discipline and effectively disseminate research findings through technical reports, presentations, and peer-reviewed papers; and
- lead high-performing research and development teams, divisions, and corporations.

> (Sidebar) The online Doctor of Engineering in Engineering program incorporates the rigor of a Ph.D. with a focus of career progression from academic research to leadership in a commercial setting.

## A2. General Information

Penn State's College of Engineering offers a Doctor of Engineering (D.Eng.) program aimed at the working professionals who desire to advance their career by demonstrating the ability to conduct and lead applied research within an industrial, military, organizational, or governmental setting. Throughout this Handbook, when "industry" is used we are also referring to military, government, and other organizational settings. To accommodate the working professional, the program is offered online through the highly regarded Penn State World Campus.

The program consists of 45 credits, of which 9 credits are focused on developing leadership and innovation management skills of value to the student's career growth, 6 credits are focused on developing research skills, and 15 credits are focused on developing the needed technical skills through graduate-level technical courses relevant for addressing an appropriate applied research topic. An additional 15 research credits are required to complete the student's praxis research. The praxis consists of original research and scholarly inquiry, with a high level of rigor as it reflects the advanced nature of doctoral studies and the expectation for the praxis to contribute new knowledge. Program milestones include the qualifying exam, comprehensive exam (praxis proposal and presentation), and final praxis and defense.

It is expected that the student will enter the doctoral program with an identified applied research topic that will result in their praxis. The praxis research topic will be finalized in consultation with a program administrator, the student, and the praxis committee, which will include the faculty advisor and other graduate faculty. The praxis research is analogous to the dissertation research that is a requirement of a Ph.D. program and the written praxis is the final written documentation in the D.Eng. program.

### Safety and Compliance

As D.Eng. students will be performing the majority of their praxis research using facilities and resources available to them within their home organizations, they should follow any and all safety and compliance protocols established by their home organization.

### Guidance for Writing the Statement of Professional Goals

This statement should be concise and well-written, and reflect your academic interests, research/praxis plan, and motivation, as well as how these further your career objectives. Please include the following sections in your statement:

**Academic Interests (approx. 1/2 page):** Discuss your academic interest in the D.Eng. program. In addition, propose a course work plan (i.e., review the program's electives and discuss which courses would be most effective to help reach your praxis research and professional goals).

**Praxis Research Interests and Rational [sic] (approx. 1 page):** The praxis research should apply what you come to the program with and will learn in this program (methods/technology/theory) to research and solve a significant/complex problem from your work. The goal of this section is to describe to the admissions committee a problem you have identified and the research plan, goals, and deliverables, and how these will be supported. In other words, this section should reflect your ability to effectively communicate the maturity of your research aspirations.

This section should include:

- Problem motivation (i.e., background of the problem/how do you know this is a problem that should be addressed via the praxis?)
- Problem statement/hypothesis (i.e., one or two sentences stating what specific problem this praxis research is going to solve)
- Project purpose (i.e., describe how the praxis research will generally address the problem)
- Project plan goals (i.e., a bulleted list of what will be analyzed, designed/researched, and implemented)
- Project deliverables (i.e., what could ultimately be delivered as the solution to this problem, in addition to the required written praxis)

**Career Objectives (approx. 1/2 page):** This section should communicate a synergy between the education and praxis research accomplished in this program with your career/professional goals.

### Advising

Academic advising typically is provided by the faculty member who supervises the student's praxis research. Other members of the student's praxis committee may also serve as advisers. A newly admitted student will be assigned a temporary adviser who will provide initial advising at the beginning of their first semester. The program will work to identify a permanent praxis adviser. In most cases this should occur in the first year; however, in some cases additional time will be required. The directors of the D.Eng. program are available to consult and assist with questions regarding the program. Upon entrance into the program, students should be working to meet the criteria to sit for the qualifying exam. The program recommends that students complete one of the following courses in their first two semesters: STAT 500, STAT 501, STAT 505, STAT 510, or ENGR 800. Students are also encouraged to complete ENGR 820 within their first two semesters. Students admitted with provisional status must comply with all conditions of their admission.

If a student has been admitted provisionally to the program or if a student's D.Eng. GPA falls below a 3.2, advisor assignment may be delayed.

**Advisor Matching Process:** The program identifies faculty members whose expertise aligns with a student's proposed research interests. The program then shares relevant portions of the student's application with potential faculty advisors. If a faculty member is interested in advising the student, the program emails the student to arrange a virtual introduction. If, following the meeting, both the student and the faculty member agree to move forward, the program documents the advisor match in LionPATH.

It is the student's responsibility to monitor their Penn State email for advisor introduction messages and to respond promptly. Delays in responding may result in missed advising opportunities.

### Qualifying Exam, Comprehensive Exam, and Final Praxis

This section discusses the three major milestones of the D.Eng. Program: Qualifying Examination, Comprehensive Examination (i.e., praxis written proposal and oral defense), and Final Written Praxis and Final Oral Defense. Note: Students must be enrolled the semester any of these milestones are attempted.

Admission to the D.Eng. Program does not imply admission to D.Eng. candidacy, which is granted only by the D.Eng. Graduate Committee after the student completes all the D.Eng. qualification procedures. The decision for or against completion of these requirements is reported to The Graduate School. Positive recommendations are entered on the transcript, but failures are not recorded.

#### Qualifying Exam

The D.Eng. Qualifying Examination is prepared and administered by the Qualifying Examination Committee comprised of members of the D.Eng. Graduate Faculty, working closely with the D.Eng. Programs Office. It is structured around an assessment of the student's proficiency with applied engineering research methods (ENGR 820), statistics, and the review and critical assessment of literature that is related to the student's proposed praxis research. The exam will consist of an online written exam taken with oversight of online proctoring.

The primary purpose of the Qualifying Examination is to provide an early assessment of whether the student is developing the knowledge, skills, and attributes the D.Eng. program has defined in its Objectives, including evidence of critical thinking skills necessary for successful applied research at the doctoral level. The Qualifying Examination is conducted early in a student's program to ensure that the considerable investment of time, resources, and effort required by the student has a high likelihood of leading to completion of the D.Eng. degree.

The exam is administered twice each academic year: once each during the fall and spring semesters. Exam dates will be announced by the D.Eng. Program Director by email to all D.Eng. students. The Qualifying Examination will occur on a Saturday, on which day there will be a 10-hour window during which the student will take two exam parts: Part 1 (2 hours) and Part 2 (4 hours). The student may take a break between exams as long as both parts are completed within the 9 hours [sic: the same paragraph says "10-hour window"]. Details for scheduling the Qualifying Examination will be included in the Qualifying Exam announcement. Should the student fail their first attempt at the Qualifying Examination, the D.Eng. Qualifying Exam Committee will determine the conditions to be met before a second (and final) examination attempt is to be administered. Each part of the exam will receive either a Pass or Fail designation.

**Eligibility:** To be eligible to take the Qualifying Examination the student must have:

- Earned at least 12 credits in courses eligible to be counted toward the D.Eng. degree (ENGR 820, STAT 5XX/ENGR 800, and two courses of technical electives) while a D.Eng. graduate student or the equivalent as determined and documented by the program;
- A GPA of 3.20 or higher for courses taken at Penn State while a D.Eng. graduate student. Note: Penn State Graduate School GPA is cumulative. If you have completed Penn State graduate courses prior to entering the D.Eng. program, your D.Eng. GPA will need to be hand calculated; and
- No incompletes, deferred grades, or transferred credits apply.

The first attempt at the D.Eng. Qualifying Examination must be taken within 25 months of matriculation into the D.Eng. program. Students must judiciously select courses such that they are eligible to take the Qualifying Examination by this deadline.

**Exam Scheduling and Preparation Period:** Two months prior to the scheduled examination, students will meet with their praxis adviser to select three exemplar peer-reviewed journal articles that are related to their praxis research area. These articles will be sent to the D.Eng. Qualification Examination Committee to identify one additional broadly-related article that will be used during Part 2 of the exam. To prepare for Part 2 of the exam a question pool will be provided to assist in preparing for the exam.

**Exam Format:** The Qualifying Examination will consist of two parts of equal weight.

Students are allowed to use handwritten or typed notes (printed, not electronic) during the exam.

**Part 1:** This part of the exam assesses the student's mastery of the material drawn from ENGR 820 (with support from the statistics course). Be prepared to use the statistical software of your choice.

**Part 2:** Each student will be given one paper (not one of the three provided) broadly related to their field of study. During this exam part, the student will have access to the Penn State Libraries to access scholarly literature as needed, but should be aware that plagiarism in any form will not be tolerated and will result in University-level sanctions that may include expulsion from the University.

In the written exam, students will be instructed to complete a series of written prompts (a subset of the question pool) to demonstrate understanding of the articles, applied research methodology, technical proficiency, and mastery of written English. The depth, quality, and accuracy of the answers to the questions will be used to assess technical mastery, and the quality of writing will be used to assess written communication skills.

When completing the written prompts, students should explain relevant theoretical underpinnings of the work, novel methodologies or techniques introduced, and significant findings leveraging visual evidence (graphics from the work, external sources, or original) to explain their perspectives of the work to an audience of faculty members.

Students are expected to explicitly cover the following topics:

- **Theoretical Background:** Succinctly demonstrate an understanding of the underlying theories that support and motivate the work.
- **Research Objectives/Methodology:** Articulate a clear understanding of the overarching objectives of the work. Describe if/how the methods proposed are appropriate given the research objectives.
- **Findings:** Clearly summarize the key findings of the work.
- **Impact:** Contextualize the impact of the work in the field leveraging literature to support your arguments and note any next steps that may/may not be associated with your proposed praxis research.

**Student Resources:** Please see Qualifying Exam Prep Guidance [https://www.sedi.psu.edu/assets/docs/DEng-Qual-Exam-Prep-Guidance.pdf]. In addition, students are advised to study foundational D.Eng. material and to master relevant coursework before taking the D.Eng. Qualifying Examination (note: you may not have access to past course material in Canvas—plan accordingly). They should also work with their faculty advisers and ENGR 820 instructor to practice accessing and citing literature (in general), synthesizing literature, discussing technical papers, and critically reviewing technical papers.

The Qualifying Examination normally will be scheduled at the end of the second week of classes during the Fall and Spring semesters.

Students who do not pass the Qualifying Examination must take it again the next time it is offered. Students who are unsuccessful in their second attempt will be disqualified from the D.Eng. program.

#### Appeals

If the Qualifying Examination is failed on the second attempt, the D.Eng. Graduate Committee will consider student petitions giving special attention to inputs from the student's area faculty and praxis adviser. The primary criteria that the committee will consider will be based on evidence of quality of accomplished research and potential for future research. This evidence should include statements by at least one and preferably two professors commenting on past and expected research, as well as a list of papers published or accepted for publication in refereed journals or conferences. The primary evidence can be strengthened by a statement of willingness of a faculty member to provide continued supervision of the student's praxis research. The committee will also consider GPA, core course and technical course grades, written and/or oral qualifying exam scores, and improvement in performance from the previous exam. Proximity to the pass threshold is not by itself sufficient grounds for reversing a decision. The decision of the committee is final.

The form to document the praxis committee can be found in the D.Eng. Resources SharePoint. Email the completed form to the D.Eng. office (DEngGradOffice@psu.edu). The chair of the committee (generally the praxis adviser) must be on graduate faculty within the D.Eng. in Engineering Program (this list of graduate faculty [https://secure.gradsch.psu.edu/gpms] is maintained by the Graduate School). Two other committee members are selected by the adviser and must be graduate faculty. With approval from D.Eng. Director and The Graduate School, additional committee members may be added, including from outside Penn State (known as a "Special Member"), if appropriate and contingent that they satisfy the requirements for special committee member as outlined by The Graduate School. The D.Eng. Program will consider forwarding requests for special membership provided the member being considered holds a doctorate. Please review the Graduate School policy for further information: GCAC-702 - Professional Doctoral Committee Composition - Professional Doctorate [https://gradschool.psu.edu/graduate-education-policies/gcac/gcac-700/gcac-702-professional-doctoral-committee-composition/].

#### Comprehensive Examination

The D.Eng. Comprehensive Examination consists of a written praxis proposal and an oral examination with presentation of the proposed praxis. Though the nature of the praxis proposal is under the jurisdiction of the doctoral committee, it is recommended that the main body of the proposal be limited to the equivalent of fifteen (15) single-spaced pages of text. This proposal should contain, as a minimum, the background and motivation for the praxis research being undertaken, the specific problem(s) to be tackled, and the approach as well as methods to be adopted for attempting the solution together with a summary of any preliminary results. Any additional material that does not belong to the core of the proposal but provides either justification of the proposed scheme or documentation of preliminary efforts, could be included in an appendix.

The Comprehensive Examination may be scheduled once the student has completed 12 of the required 15 credits of technical electives. In addition, the graduate school must be notified of the Comprehensive Exam date no later than 2 weeks prior. This is done by emailing the completed form, found in the D.Eng. Resources SharePoint, to DEngGradOffice@psu.edu. Please make sure the following information: date, location (in-person or online), and time is included on the form. If the exam is online, send a link created by the advisor. Please review the Graduate School policy for further information: GCAC-706 Comprehensive Examination - Professional Doctorate [https://gradschool.psu.edu/graduate-education-policies/gcac/gcac-700/gcac-706-comprehensive-examination-professional-doctorate/].

Prior to the Comprehensive Exam, the committee members will receive an evaluation form to fill out after the exam indicating results. This form is then returned to the Graduate School. The results of the exam will be provided to the student along with any recommendations, which may include recommendations for additional coursework, topics for review, etc.

**Annual Assessment of Student Progress:** Following successful completion of the Comprehensive Examination, students are responsible for completing and submitting the Annual Assessment of Student Progress form to the D.Eng. Program Office by the end of the first month of the Fall semester until degree completion. The form is available in the program's SharePoint folder. It is the student's responsibility to ensure the form is completed and submitted by the required deadline.

#### Final Written Praxis and Final Oral Defense

The written praxis must be provided to the members of the student's D.Eng. praxis committee at least 2 weeks prior and the final oral defense must be scheduled with The Graduate School through the D.Eng. Program Staff Assistant at least 3 weeks prior to the exam date.

The D.Eng. program requires a format review of the written praxis to ensure that the written praxis will meet the requirements as outlined by the program. The D.Eng. will follow the dates for "thesis or dissertation for format review" provided by the Graduate School; however, the process is different. The written praxis should be emailed to DEngGradOffice@psu.edu and feedback of any required changes will be provided. Note, use of templates provided (see below) is highly recommended to ensure compliance with the formatting guidelines.

The final oral defense must be completed within 8 years of entrance into the D.Eng. program. A minimum of three months must have elapsed since the Comprehensive Examination. At least 3 weeks before the final oral defense, the student must see the D.Eng. Program Staff Assistant for the forms necessary to officially schedule the final oral defense. One of the forms the student will receive will be verification by the adviser's signature that the praxis is ready to be distributed to the other committee members and ready to be defended. At least 2 weeks before the defense date, the student will distribute copies of the written praxis to the committee members. The defense may be canceled if documents are not delivered to committee members at least 2 weeks prior to the defense date. The graduate staff coordinator will verify that all requirements have been met and will obtain the D.Eng. Director's signature and send the request on to the Graduate School.

The Graduate School sends the Final Oral Exam Report form to the D.Eng. Program Staff Assistant indicating that the defense has been officially scheduled. This examination, open to the public, relates in large measure to the praxis but may cover the entire field of study. The exam may take place in person, virtually, or in hybrid format. If online or hybrid, the meeting link must be created by the advisor. The praxis committee determines the exact examination procedure. All members of the doctoral praxis must participate in the final oral defense. All committee members' marked evaluation of the defense will be recorded electronically and submitted to The Graduate School. A favorable vote of at least two-thirds of the members of the committee is required for passing. If the student fails, it is the responsibility of the praxis committee to determine whether or not another examination may be taken. The approval of the praxis rests entirely with the doctoral committee and the D.Eng. program director.

Final oral defenses and written praxis submissions must meet The Graduate School's published deadlines [https://gradschool.psu.edu/academics/academic-dates-and-deadlines] for completion and be submitted by the published dates for graduation in a given semester. The written praxis must also be signed by the Director of the D.Eng. program, ensuring that it meets the quality and rigor expectations of the D.Eng. program. It is incumbent on the student to ensure that the praxis advisor, committee members, and the D.Eng. director have sufficient time to provide feedback such the that required deadlines can be met. D.Eng. deadlines, as published by The Graduate School, are not flexible under any circumstance.

### Other Requirements

#### Communication and Language Competence

D.Eng. students shall demonstrate competence in the use of the English language for purposes of both written and oral communication. They should be able to communicate technical material in a clear, concise, and well-organized manner. The praxis adviser or the doctoral committee may require that the student take formal courses in technical writing, speech, etc., if it is determined that the communication skills are inadequate. There will be various communication experiences throughout the program.

#### Writing Requirements

Before the D.Eng. student's Comprehensive Examination is scheduled, the praxis adviser must formally attest to the English competence of the student.

Students are expected to gain writing experience by preparing research reports, conference papers, and refereed journal articles as they report on their original findings. The doctoral committee is expected to review and critically evaluate any of the student's written work, including the praxis proposal, interim reports, and manuscripts.

The written praxis is to meet the standards set forth by The Graduate School and is to be evaluated by the doctoral committee for the quality of the writing as well as technical content. At the beginning of the research, each student should access the Thesis and Dissertation Guide [https://gradschool.psu.edu/academics/theses-and-dissertations] to ensure that the written or graphical materials generated from time to time can be in the format acceptable to The Graduate School.

It is strongly recommended that D.Eng. students use the LaTeX style or Word template provided via the link above to ensure compliance with formatting guidelines.

#### Oral and Presentation Requirements

Presentation skills of all D.Eng. students will be evaluated after formal completion of all procedures required for admission to D.Eng. candidacy. Each student will make a 15-to-20-minute presentation on a technical topic to a group of students and/or faculty including their praxis adviser. This presentation will be coordinated by the praxis adviser and should take place no later than the first semester after passing the Qualifying Examination and prior to the Comprehensive Exam. Students found to have deficiencies in presentation skills will be required to take a course to develop these skills. The form to document the successful completion of this requirement should be completed and emailed to the DEngGradOffice@psu.edu. The form can be found in the D.Eng. Resource SharePoint.

In addition, the student will make an oral presentation of approximately 30 minutes in length to the doctoral committee on the praxis problem and the approach to its solution. This presentation of the praxis proposal is a part of the Comprehensive Examination.

The student is encouraged to attend conferences to make oral presentations of their praxis research work and then write papers for submission to journals. These opportunities are discussed in consultation with the praxis adviser.

Finally, an oral presentation on the results of the praxis research is required. This presentation is part of the final praxis defense.

#### Submitting Intent to Graduate

Students who intend to graduate should follow the University's process: https://www.registrar.psu.edu/graduation/intent.cfm

In addition, the D.Eng. program requires the following completed documents: DEng Residency Requirement and Program Milestones form, the DEng Praxis Approval Form & DEng Annual Assessment of Student Progress form, and a completed Program Requirements Checklist form, which outlines all required program completion requirements. These must be completed and submitted to the D.Eng. program as part of the graduation application process. All forms are available in the D.Eng. Resource SharePoint.

### Other Constraints

To be eligible for the Comprehensive and Final Oral Exams, a student must have a minimum grade point average of 3.0 (within the D.Eng. program), must be registered, must have no missing or deferred grades, and must have satisfied any provisional requirements for admission. In addition, course completed with a grade of D of F [sic: D or F] will not count toward degree requirements but are part of the GPA calculation. Students who fail to register will be assessed tuition for any semesters in which they did not register before being permitted to continue their D.Eng. studies.

### D.Eng. Residency Requirement and Time Limit

Five days of residency during the length of their program is required of all D.Eng. students and can be satisfied at any Penn State campus or combination of campuses (except World Campus). The full residency requirement can be met by attending a single five-day campus residency or multiple shorter on-campus residencies that combine to a minimum of five days. The D.Eng. program office will track days and certify this requirement has or will be met (i.e., if the student intends to defend in person) by the date of the Final Praxis Defense.

Immediately prior to each fall semester, the D.Eng. Program will hold an optional two-day orientation on the Penn State University Park campus that can count toward part of the residency requirement. Residency days can also be obtained through visits to campus for research meetings with praxis faculty, students, and researchers; attendance in lab group meetings; on-campus workshops; and organized D.Eng. Program–specific professional development activities that will be offered from time to time for those in the program and to the broader community. Some of these days may also be spent in fulfillment of major program milestones including your qualifying exam, comprehensive exam, and praxis defense.

A doctoral student must complete the program and submit an accepted praxis within 8 years of matriculation into the D.Eng. Program.

It is required that D.Eng. students maintain continuous enrollment from the first semester they are accepted (i.e., a Fall start if accepted for Fall, a Summer start if accepted for Summer) until completion of the program. A discontinued student must then re-apply to the D.Eng. Program (see Resume Study [https://gradschool.psu.edu/admissions/change-or-resume-study]). Readmission is not guaranteed, as the application will be evaluated as a new application alongside the current applicant pool.

### Requesting a Leave of Absence

The D.Eng. program is designed for and requires steady, continuous progress. As such, students are expected to remain actively engaged each term. Consistent participation and forward momentum are essential to completing the program in a timely manner and to achieving strong outcomes in the praxis.

That said, we recognize that rare, extenuating professional or personal circumstances may arise. In such cases, a Leave of Absence (LoA) may be considered, but it is not intended as a routine mechanism for managing workload or scheduling challenges. Students may be approved for one LoA during the program and an LoA, if approved, is typically limited to one semester. Because a leave can disrupt academic progress and praxis research continuity, any request requires advance planning and formal approval from both the D.Eng. program, the Graduate School, and the student's praxis advisor (if applicable). Students on an approved LoA are expected to remain in appropriate communication with their praxis advisor and, when feasible, continue progress on praxis planning or research activities consistent with program and Graduate School policies.

To initiate the LoA process, please request and complete the LoA form prior to the start of the semester in which you wish to take the LoA. Submit a copy of the form to the D.Eng. Administrative Support Assistant (after consultation with your adviser and/or committee chair). Also, provide the information request below in your email.

1. The reason for the request;
2. Specific start and end date of Leave of Absence; and
3. A written summary of the remaining requirements for the degree.

The D.Eng. Administrative Support Assistant, will create a memo with the information provided and will obtain a statement indicating whether or not the D.Eng. Program Head supports the request. Once signed, the request will be submitted in the Graduate Request Management System (GRMS). A Leave of Absence request is not complete until all information is received and meets final approval.

## B1. 工程学工程博士项目目标

宾州州立大学（Penn State）长期以来备受尊重，并一贯被公认为拥有全美顶尖的工程项目之一。学校很荣幸通过 Penn State World Campus 提供高质量的在线工程学工程博士（D.Eng.）项目。该在线 D.Eng. 项目由学术背景扎实、具有真实业界经验的知名宾州州立大学教师授课，可帮助你晋升至私营和公共部门中研究与工程领域的最高领导岗位。作为 D.Eng. 学生，你可以学会：

- 研究和开发能够惠及工业、政府或军事机构的新产品或新工艺；
- 在本学科内——并在适当时跨越多个学科——分析与综合关键信息；
- 阐述本学科的主要问题，并通过技术报告、演讲和同行评审论文有效传播研究成果；以及
- 领导高绩效的研发团队、部门和企业。

> （侧栏）在线工程学工程博士项目兼具 Ph.D. 的严谨性，同时聚焦于从学术研究走向商业环境中领导岗位的职业发展。

## B2. 总体信息

宾州州立大学工程学院开设工程博士（D.Eng.）项目，面向希望通过展示自己在工业、军事、组织或政府环境中开展并领导应用研究的能力来推动职业发展的在职专业人士。在本手册中，凡使用"产业/工业（industry）"一词时，也同时指军事、政府及其他组织环境。为照顾在职人士，本项目通过声誉卓著的 Penn State World Campus 以在线方式提供。

本项目共 45 学分：其中 9 学分侧重培养对学生职业成长有价值的领导力与创新管理能力；6 学分侧重培养研究能力；15 学分通过与恰当应用研究课题相关的研究生级技术课程，培养所需的技术能力。另需 15 个研究学分以完成学生的 praxis 研究。Praxis 由原创研究和学术探究构成，具有高度严谨性，这体现了博士学习的高阶性质，以及对 praxis 贡献新知识的期望。项目里程碑包括资格考试、综合考试（praxis 开题报告与陈述）以及最终 praxis 与答辩。

项目期望学生在进入博士项目时已确定一个将形成其 praxis 的应用研究课题。Praxis 研究课题将由项目管理人员、学生以及 praxis 委员会（包括指导教师和其他研究生导师）共同商议后最终确定。Praxis 研究类似于 Ph.D. 项目所要求的博士论文研究，而书面 praxis 是 D.Eng. 项目最终的书面成果文件。

### 职业目标陈述写作指引

该陈述应简明、行文流畅，体现你的学术兴趣、研究/praxis 计划和动机，以及这些如何推进你的职业目标。请在陈述中包含以下部分：

**学术兴趣（约 1/2 页）：** 阐述你对 D.Eng. 项目的学术兴趣。此外，提出一份选课计划（即查看本项目的选修课，并说明哪些课程最能有效帮助你实现 praxis 研究和职业目标）。

**Praxis 研究兴趣与理由（约 1 页）：**〔原文 "Rational" 为 "Rationale" 笔误〕Praxis 研究应运用你入学时已具备的以及将在本项目中学到的（方法/技术/理论），去研究并解决你工作中的一个重大/复杂问题。本部分的目的是向招生委员会说明你所发现的问题、研究计划、目标和交付成果，以及这些将如何获得支持。换言之，本部分应体现你有效传达自身研究志向成熟度的能力。

本部分应包括：

- 问题动机（即问题背景／你如何知道这是一个应通过 praxis 解决的问题？）
- 问题陈述/假设（即用一两句话说明本 praxis 研究将解决的具体问题）
- 项目目的（即说明 praxis 研究将如何总体上解决该问题）
- 项目计划目标（即以要点列表列出将要分析、设计/研究和实施的内容）
- 项目交付成果（即除必需的书面 praxis 之外，最终可作为该问题解决方案交付的内容）

**职业目标（约 1/2 页）：** 本部分应说明在本项目中完成的学习与 praxis 研究，与你的职业/专业目标之间的协同关系。

### 导师指导（Advising）

学术指导通常由指导学生 praxis 研究的教师提供。学生 praxis 委员会的其他成员也可担任指导者。新录取学生将被分配一名临时导师，在其第一学期开始时提供初期指导。项目会努力为学生确定一名正式（永久）praxis 导师。大多数情况下这应在第一年内完成；但在某些情况下需要更多时间。D.Eng. 项目的主任可就项目相关问题提供咨询和协助。入学后，学生就应着手满足参加资格考试的条件。项目建议学生在前两个学期内完成以下课程之一：STAT 500、STAT 501、STAT 505、STAT 510 或 ENGR 800。同时鼓励学生在前两个学期内完成 ENGR 820。以有条件（provisional）身份录取的学生必须遵守其录取的全部条件。

如果学生是有条件录取，或学生的 D.Eng. GPA 低于 3.2，导师分配可能会被推迟。

**导师匹配流程：** 项目会找出专长与学生拟定研究兴趣相符的教师，然后将学生申请材料的相关部分分享给潜在的指导教师。如果某位教师有意指导该学生，项目会发邮件给学生安排一次线上见面介绍。见面后，若学生与该教师双方都同意继续，项目会在 LionPATH 中记录这一导师匹配。

学生有责任留意自己的 Penn State 邮箱中的导师介绍邮件并及时回复。回复延迟可能导致错失导师匹配机会。

请注意，课程结束后可能无法再在 Canvas 中访问课程资料，包括 ENGR 820 的资料。如果你预计需要课程内容来准备资格考试或支持 praxis 研究，请提前做好安排。

### 课程学分转入

根据研究生院政策 GCAC 309《转入学分》，任何已从宾州州立大学或其他院校获授硕士学位的学生，均不得将已授予硕士学位中的任何学分转入 D.Eng. 项目。如果你有"未使用"的学分，最多 9 学分的高质量研究生课程可考虑转入 D.Eng. 项目，前提是不超过 5 年。学生需准备一份供评估的材料包，内容包括：显示该（些）课程成绩的成绩单；能说明拟转入课程未被用于满足学位要求的学位要求说明；希望转入的课程，以及（如有）对应的宾州州立大学课程。

在宾州州立大学以非学位（nonmatriculated）和/或证书学生身份修读的课程，若在最近 5 年内修读，经项目批准，最多 15 学分可纳入 D.Eng. 项目。

请注意，是否审核你的研究生成绩单并认定可转入或可纳入的学分，由 D.Eng. 项目酌情决定。

### 获取研究生证书

学生可以考虑在 D.Eng. 学习过程中获取一个研究生证书。为此，学生必须申请并被证书项目录取，而且必须在修完该证书全部必修课之前完成申请，即证书不能追溯申请。

### 资格考试、综合考试与最终 Praxis

本节讨论 D.Eng. 项目的三大里程碑：资格考试、综合考试（即 praxis 书面开题报告与口头答辩），以及最终书面 praxis 与最终口头答辩。注意：学生在尝试任一里程碑的那个学期必须处于注册状态。

被 D.Eng. 项目录取并不意味着获得 D.Eng. 博士候选人（candidacy）资格；候选人资格只有在学生完成全部 D.Eng. 资格程序后，由 D.Eng. 研究生委员会授予。关于是否完成这些要求的决定会报送研究生院。通过的结论会记入成绩单，但未通过不会被记录。

#### 资格考试（Qualifying Exam）

D.Eng. 资格考试由 D.Eng. 研究生导师组成的资格考试委员会命题和组织，并与 D.Eng. 项目办公室密切合作。考试围绕以下方面评估学生的能力：应用工程研究方法（ENGR 820）、统计学，以及对与学生拟定 praxis 研究相关文献的综述与批判性评估。考试形式为在线笔试，并有在线监考。

资格考试的主要目的是尽早评估学生是否正在培养 D.Eng. 项目在其目标中所界定的知识、技能和素质，包括在博士层次成功开展应用研究所必需的批判性思维能力的证据。资格考试安排在学生学业的早期进行，以确保学生所需投入的大量时间、资源和精力有很大可能带来 D.Eng. 学位的完成。

考试每学年举行两次：秋季和春季学期各一次。考试日期将由 D.Eng. 项目主任通过电子邮件通知所有 D.Eng. 学生。资格考试在某个周六举行，当天有一个 10 小时的时间窗口，学生在此期间完成两部分考试：第一部分（2 小时）和第二部分（4 小时）。只要两部分在 9 小时内完成，学生可以在两部分之间休息。〔原文同段先写"10 小时窗口"后写"9 小时内"，存在前后不一致，以考试通知为准〕资格考试的具体排期细节将包含在资格考试通知中。如果学生第一次资格考试未通过，D.Eng. 资格考试委员会将确定在第二次（也是最后一次）考试前须满足的条件。每部分考试的结果为"通过"或"不通过"。

**参考资格：** 学生须满足以下条件方可参加资格考试：

- 在作为 D.Eng. 研究生期间，已修得至少 12 学分可计入 D.Eng. 学位的课程（ENGR 820、STAT 5XX/ENGR 800，以及两门技术选修课），或经项目认定并记录的同等学分；
- 作为 D.Eng. 研究生期间在宾州州立大学所修课程的 GPA 达到 3.20 或以上。注意：宾州州立大学研究生院的 GPA 是累计计算的。如果你在进入 D.Eng. 项目之前已修过宾州州立大学的研究生课程，你的 D.Eng. GPA 需要手工计算；以及
- 不得有未完成（incomplete）、延期成绩（deferred grade），转入学分也不计入。

D.Eng. 资格考试的首次参考必须在入学（matriculation）后 25 个月内进行。学生必须审慎选课，以确保在此截止前具备参考资格。

**考试排期与准备期：** 在预定考试日期前两个月，学生将与其 praxis 导师会面，选出三篇与其 praxis 研究领域相关的典范性同行评审期刊论文。这些论文将提交给 D.Eng. 资格考试委员会，由委员会另选一篇与之大致相关的论文，用于考试第二部分。为准备第二部分考试，项目将提供一个题库以协助备考。

**考试形式：** 资格考试由权重相同的两部分组成。

考试期间允许使用手写或打印的笔记（纸质，不可使用电子版）。

**第一部分：** 本部分评估学生对 ENGR 820 内容（辅以统计课程内容）的掌握程度。请准备好使用你自选的统计软件。

**第二部分：** 每位学生将拿到一篇与其研究领域大致相关的论文（不是所提交三篇中的任何一篇）。在这部分考试中，学生可以根据需要访问宾州州立大学图书馆获取学术文献，但应清楚：任何形式的抄袭都不会被容忍，并将导致大学层面的处分，可能包括开除学籍。

在笔试中，学生将被要求完成一系列书面题目（题库中的一个子集），以展示对论文、应用研究方法、技术能力以及书面英语的掌握。答案的深度、质量和准确性将用于评估技术掌握程度，写作质量将用于评估书面沟通能力。

在完成书面题目时，学生应解释该研究相关的理论基础、所引入的新方法或技术，以及重要发现，并借助可视化证据（论文中的图表、外部来源或自制图示）向教师读者阐述自己对该研究的看法。

学生应明确涵盖以下主题：

- **理论背景：** 简明地展示对支撑和驱动该研究的基础理论的理解。
- **研究目标/方法：** 清晰阐述对该研究总体目标的理解。说明所提出的方法在给定研究目标下是否恰当、以及如何恰当。
- **研究发现：** 清楚概括该研究的主要发现。
- **影响：** 借助文献支持你的论点，将该研究在领域中的影响置于背景中加以阐述，并指出可能与/可能不与你拟定的 praxis 研究相关的后续步骤。

**学生资源：** 请参阅《资格考试备考指引》（Qualifying Exam Prep Guidance）。此外，建议学生在参加 D.Eng. 资格考试前学习 D.Eng. 的基础资料并掌握相关课程内容（注意：你可能无法再在 Canvas 中访问以往课程资料——请提前安排）。学生还应与其指导教师及 ENGR 820 授课教师合作，练习（一般性的）文献检索与引用、文献综合、技术论文讨论以及技术论文的批判性评审。

资格考试通常安排在秋季和春季学期上课第二周的周末（第二周结束时）。

未通过资格考试的学生必须在下一次考试时重考。第二次仍未通过的学生将被取消 D.Eng. 项目资格。

#### 申诉

如果资格考试第二次未通过，D.Eng. 研究生委员会将受理学生的申诉，并特别重视学生所在领域教师和 praxis 导师的意见。委员会考虑的主要标准是已完成研究的质量和未来研究潜力的证据。这些证据应包括至少一位、最好两位教授对学生过去及预期研究的评述，以及已在同行评审期刊或会议上发表或已被接收发表的论文清单。如有教师书面表示愿意继续指导该学生的 praxis 研究，可加强上述主要证据。委员会还将考虑 GPA、核心课和技术课成绩、笔试和/或口试的资格考试分数，以及相较上一次考试的进步。仅仅接近及格线本身不足以成为推翻决定的理由。委员会的决定为最终决定。

#### Praxis 委员会的任命

学生的 D.Eng. praxis 委员会必须不晚于成功通过 D.Eng. 资格考试那个学期的期末任命。用于登记 praxis 委员会的表格可在 D.Eng. Resources SharePoint 中找到。将填好的表格通过邮件发送至 D.Eng. 办公室（DEngGradOffice@psu.edu）。委员会主席（通常为 praxis 导师）必须是 D.Eng. 工程项目内的研究生导师（graduate faculty）（该研究生导师名单由研究生院维护）。另外两名委员由导师选定，且必须是研究生导师。经 D.Eng. 主任和研究生院批准，可在适当情况下增补委员，包括宾州州立大学以外的人员（称为"特别委员"，Special Member），前提是其满足研究生院规定的特别委员要求。若被考虑的人员持有博士学位，D.Eng. 项目将考虑转呈特别委员申请。更多信息请查阅研究生院政策：GCAC-702《专业博士委员会组成——专业博士》。

#### 综合考试（Comprehensive Examination）

D.Eng. 综合考试由书面 praxis 开题报告和一场包含拟定 praxis 陈述的口试组成。尽管 praxis 开题报告的具体形式由博士委员会决定，但建议开题报告正文篇幅限制在相当于十五（15）页单倍行距文字。开题报告至少应包含：所开展 praxis 研究的背景与动机、拟解决的具体问题，以及拟采用的求解思路和方法，并附上任何初步结果的摘要。任何不属于开题报告核心、但可为所提方案提供论证或记录前期工作的补充材料，可放入附录。

学生在修完所需 15 学分技术选修课中的 12 学分后，即可安排综合考试。此外，必须不晚于考试前 2 周将综合考试日期通知研究生院。具体做法是：将 D.Eng. Resources SharePoint 中的表格填好后，发送邮件至 DEngGradOffice@psu.edu。请确保表格中包含以下信息：日期、地点（线下或线上）和时间。如果考试在线上进行，请发送由导师创建的会议链接。更多信息请查阅研究生院政策：GCAC-706《综合考试——专业博士》。

综合考试前，委员会成员将收到一份评估表，在考试后填写并注明结果。该表随后交回研究生院。考试结果将连同任何建议一并告知学生，建议可能包括补修课程、需复习的主题等。

**学生进展年度评估：** 成功通过综合考试后，学生有责任在每年秋季学期第一个月结束前，向 D.Eng. 项目办公室填写并提交《学生进展年度评估》表，直至完成学位。该表可在项目的 SharePoint 文件夹中获取。确保表格按规定截止日期完成并提交是学生自己的责任。

#### 最终书面 Praxis 与最终口头答辩

书面 praxis 必须至少提前 2 周提供给学生 D.Eng. praxis 委员会的成员；最终口头答辩必须至少在考试日期前 3 周通过 D.Eng. 项目行政助理（Program Staff Assistant）向研究生院预约排期。

D.Eng. 项目要求对书面 praxis 进行格式审查，以确保其符合项目规定的要求。D.Eng. 将遵循研究生院公布的"学位论文格式审查"日期，但流程不同。书面 praxis 应通过邮件发送至 DEngGradOffice@psu.edu，随后会收到需修改之处的反馈。注意：强烈建议使用所提供的模板（见下文），以确保符合格式规范。

最终口头答辩必须在进入 D.Eng. 项目后 8 年内完成，且距综合考试至少已过三个月。在最终口头答辩前至少 3 周，学生须找 D.Eng. 项目行政助理领取正式安排答辩所需的表格。学生将收到的表格之一，是由导师签字确认 praxis 已可分发给其他委员并可进行答辩的确认书。在答辩日期前至少 2 周，学生须将书面 praxis 副本分发给委员会成员。如果未能在答辩日期前至少 2 周将文件送达委员，答辩可能会被取消。研究生事务协调员将核实所有要求均已满足，取得 D.Eng. 主任签字，并将申请转交研究生院。

研究生院会向 D.Eng. 项目行政助理发送《最终口试报告》表，表明答辩已正式排期。这场考试向公众开放，主要围绕 praxis 进行，但可能涵盖整个研究领域。考试可以线下、线上或混合形式进行。若为线上或混合形式，会议链接必须由导师创建。Praxis 委员会决定具体的考试程序。博士 praxis〔委员会〕的所有成员都必须参加最终口头答辩。所有委员对答辩的评定意见将以电子方式记录并提交研究生院。须获得委员会至少三分之二成员的赞成票方可通过。如果学生未通过，由 praxis 委员会决定是否允许再考一次。Praxis 的批准完全取决于博士委员会和 D.Eng. 项目主任。

最终口头答辩和书面 praxis 的提交必须满足研究生院公布的完成截止日期，并在当学期公布的毕业日期前提交。书面 praxis 还必须经 D.Eng. 项目主任签字，以确保其达到 D.Eng. 项目的质量与严谨性要求。学生有责任确保 praxis 导师、委员会成员和 D.Eng. 主任有足够的时间提供反馈，从而能赶上所要求的截止日期。研究生院公布的 D.Eng. 截止日期在任何情况下都不可变通。

### 其他要求

#### 沟通与语言能力

D.Eng. 学生应展示在书面和口头沟通中运用英语的能力，应能够以清晰、简洁、条理分明的方式传达技术内容。如认定学生沟通能力不足，praxis 导师或博士委员会可要求学生修读技术写作、演讲等正式课程。整个项目过程中会有各种沟通实践的机会。

#### 写作要求

在安排 D.Eng. 学生的综合考试之前，praxis 导师必须正式证明该学生的英语能力。

学生应通过撰写研究报告、会议论文和同行评审期刊文章来报告其原创发现，从而积累写作经验。博士委员会应审阅并批判性评估学生的任何书面成果，包括 praxis 开题报告、中期报告和稿件。

书面 praxis 须符合研究生院规定的标准，并由博士委员会就写作质量和技术内容进行评估。在研究开始时，每位学生都应查阅《学位论文指南》（Thesis and Dissertation Guide），以确保不时产出的文字或图表材料能采用研究生院认可的格式。

强烈建议 D.Eng. 学生使用上述链接中提供的 LaTeX 样式或 Word 模板，以确保符合格式规范。

#### 口头与陈述要求

所有 D.Eng. 学生在正式完成获得 D.Eng. 候选人资格所需的全部程序后，将接受陈述能力评估。每位学生将面向包括其 praxis 导师在内的一组学生和/或教师，就某一技术主题做 15 至 20 分钟的报告。该报告由 praxis 导师协调安排，应不晚于通过资格考试后的第一个学期、并在综合考试之前进行。被认定陈述能力存在不足的学生，将被要求修读一门提升此类能力的课程。用于记录顺利完成本要求的表格应填写后发送至 DEngGradOffice@psu.edu。该表格可在 D.Eng. Resource SharePoint 中找到。

此外，学生将向博士委员会就 praxis 问题及其求解思路做约 30 分钟的口头报告。该 praxis 开题陈述是综合考试的一部分。

鼓励学生参加学术会议，就其 praxis 研究工作做口头报告，然后撰写论文投稿期刊。这些机会可与 praxis 导师商议。

最后，需要就 praxis 研究成果做口头报告。该报告是最终 praxis 答辩的一部分。

#### 提交毕业意向（Intent to Graduate）

计划毕业的学生应遵循大学的流程：https://www.registrar.psu.edu/graduation/intent.cfm

此外，D.Eng. 项目还要求提交以下已完成的文件：《D.Eng. 驻校要求与项目里程碑》表、《D.Eng. Praxis 批准表》及《D.Eng. 学生进展年度评估》表，以及一份已完成的《项目要求核对清单》（Program Requirements Checklist）——该清单列出全部毕业所需要求。这些文件必须作为毕业申请流程的一部分填写并提交给 D.Eng. 项目。所有表格均可在 D.Eng. Resource SharePoint 中获取。

### 其他限制条件

要获得参加综合考试和最终口试的资格，学生须：D.Eng. 项目内 GPA 不低于 3.0、处于注册状态、无缺失或延期成绩，并已满足录取时的任何附加条件。此外，成绩为 D 或 F〔原文 "D of F" 为 "D or F" 笔误〕的课程不计入学位要求，但会计入 GPA 计算。更多信息请参阅研究生院 GCAC-401《评分制度》政策。通过资格考试后，学生必须在每个秋季和春季学期保持连续注册，直至完成 praxis 答辩。未注册的学生在获准继续 D.Eng. 学业之前，将被收取其未注册各学期的学费。

### D.Eng. 驻校要求与时间限制

所有 D.Eng. 学生在整个学业期间须完成五天驻校（residency），可在宾州州立大学任一校区或多个校区（World Campus 除外）累计完成。完整的驻校要求可通过参加一次为期五天的校园驻校，或多次较短、合计不少于五天的校园驻校来满足。D.Eng. 项目办公室将记录天数，并在最终 Praxis 答辩日期前认证该要求已经或将会满足（即若学生打算线下答辩）。

每年秋季学期开始前，D.Eng. 项目会在宾州州立大学 University Park 校区举办为期两天的可选迎新活动，可计入部分驻校要求。驻校天数还可通过以下方式获得：到校与 praxis 导师、学生和研究人员进行研究会议；参加实验室小组会议；参加校内工作坊；以及参加 D.Eng. 项目不定期为项目学生和更广泛群体组织的专业发展活动。其中部分天数也可用于完成主要项目里程碑，包括资格考试、综合考试和 praxis 答辩。

博士生必须在进入 D.Eng. 项目后 8 年内完成学业并提交一份被接受的 praxis。

### 知识产权

［注：本节可能变更，正在评估中］一般而言，由于 D.Eng. 学生不是宾州州立大学的雇员，且研究中不使用宾州州立大学的设施，因此适用的知识产权（IP）政策是其所在单位的政策。宾州州立大学关于学生知识产权的政策见 IPG01《教师关于学生知识产权的指引》和 IPG02《学生特别知识产权协议表》。

### 发表期望

项目期望 praxis 研究具备相应的质量、深度和严谨性，并在同行评审期刊和/或会议论文集上发表。此外，研究及由此产生的发表成果（包括书面 praxis）将通过宾州州立大学的 Scholarsphere 公开提供。虽然 D.Eng. 项目对毕业前所需的发表数量以及期刊与会议的构成没有具体要求，但这一期望将由你与 praxis 导师商定，以满足导师的期望以及你的 praxis 研究所属领域的期望。

### 连续注册

D.Eng. 学生必须从被录取的第一个学期起（即秋季录取则秋季开学，夏季录取则夏季开学）保持连续注册，直至完成项目。如果学生在某个秋季或春季学期未注册（夏季学期不算在内，除非是你的第一个学期；强烈鼓励所有 D.Eng. 学生夏季也注册），且学生并未处于事先批准的休学（LoA）状态，将被项目终止学籍（discontinued）。被终止学籍的学生之后必须重新申请 D.Eng. 项目（见"恢复学业"Resume Study）。重新录取不受保证，因为该申请将被视为新申请，与当期申请者一同评估。

### 申请休学（Leave of Absence）

D.Eng. 项目的设计以稳定、持续的进展为前提，并要求如此。因此，学生应在每个学期都保持积极投入。持续参与和不断推进，对于按时完成项目以及在 praxis 中取得优秀成果至关重要。

尽管如此，我们理解可能会出现罕见的、特殊的职业或个人情况。在这种情况下可以考虑休学（LoA），但休学并非用于处理工作量或日程冲突的常规手段。学生在整个项目期间可获批一次休学，且休学如获批准，通常限于一个学期。由于休学可能打断学业进展和 praxis 研究的连续性，任何申请都需要提前规划，并获得 D.Eng. 项目、研究生院以及学生 praxis 导师（如适用）的正式批准。获批休学的学生应与其 praxis 导师保持适当沟通，并在可行时，按照项目及研究生院政策继续推进 praxis 规划或研究活动。

如需启动休学流程，请在你希望休学的学期开始之前申请并填写休学表。将表格副本提交给 D.Eng. 行政支持助理（在与导师和/或委员会主席商议之后）。同时，请在邮件中提供以下信息：

1. 申请理由；
2. 休学的具体起止日期；以及
3. 学位剩余要求的书面摘要。

D.Eng. 行政支持助理将根据所提供的信息撰写一份备忘录，并取得一份表明 D.Eng. 项目负责人是否支持该申请的声明。签字后，申请将在研究生申请管理系统（Graduate Request Management System，GRMS）中提交。在收到全部信息并获得最终批准之前，休学申请不算完成。

## B4. 年度迎新与项目会议

秋季学期开始前，D.Eng. 项目将为在读 D.Eng. 学生举办为期 2 天的项目会议。该会议为自愿参加，在 University Park 校区举行。差旅安排及费用由学生自理。如果两天都线下参加，出勤将计入 D.Eng. 驻校要求。

如不线下参加，学生可参与部分以混合形式进行的会议环节。

## B5. 项目终止

D.Eng. 项目完全相信被录取的学生都具备成功的条件，但在某些情况下，学生可能被终止学业。因此，我们遵循 GCAC-803《因学业不合格终止研究生学位项目的程序》（现行版）——宾州州立大学 J. Jeffrey and Ann Marie Fox 研究生院。此外，如果学生未在前 25 个月内参加资格考试、两次资格考试均未通过，和/或 D.Eng. GPA 低于本手册规定的最低要求，也将被终止学业。
